# Medyanın oyunları: cards for your review

These 17 cards are **lab candidates**. None is in the production catalogue. Please read each definition and
its conditions, then tell me which to approve, edit or drop. A card reaches the site only after (1) you
approve it here and (2) it passes its lab controls.

Every card also carries these two non-examples, so they are not repeated below:

- the outlet quotes or attributes the wording to a named speaker and reports, disputes or rejects it;
- an opinion column, interview or signed commentary.

Every card ends with: *Metnin ne yaptığını tanımlar; niyet hakkında bir şey söylemez.*

## Ad hominem (Kişiye saldırı)

`ad-hominem` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

The text answers a claim or action by characterising the person who made it — their character, identity or past — in place of the claim itself.

**All of these must hold:**

1. The text refers to a claim, decision or action by an identifiable person or group.
2. The outlet's own wording characterises that person's character, identity or past.
3. The characterisation stands in for an assessment of the claim: the claim's content is not addressed in the headline or lede.

**Not this:** Reporting a relevant fact about the person that bears directly on the claim, such as a documented conflict of interest.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Causal overclaim (Kanıtsız nedensellik)

`causal-overclaim` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

The text states that one thing caused another when the supplied material shows only that they occurred together or in sequence.

**All of these must hold:**

1. The text makes an explicit causal statement (caused, led to, because of, thanks to, -dığı için, sonucunda, yüzünden).
2. The evidence given in the text or context for that link is co-occurrence or time order only.

**Not this:** A causal statement attributed to a study, court finding or official report that is named in the text.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Cherry picking (Seçmeci kanıt kullanımı)

`cherry-picking` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

The text supports a conclusion with selected indicators or dates while the supplied material contains limiting figures from the same source that it leaves out.

**All of these must hold:**

1. The text presents specific indicators, dates or cases as support for a general conclusion.
2. The supplied context contains other figures from the same source or period that limit or reverse that conclusion.
3. Those limiting figures are absent from the headline and lede.

**Not this:** A text that explicitly limits its claim to the indicator it reports and makes no general conclusion.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Consequence framing (Sonuç çerçevelemesi)

`consequence-framing` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

The headline leads with an expected or possible effect of an event in place of the event itself.

**All of these must hold:**

1. An identifiable event (a decision, statement, incident) is the news.
2. The headline's main clause states a consequence or expected effect, and the event appears only in the lede or not at all.
3. The consequence is a projection, not something reported as having already happened.

**Not this:** A headline reporting a consequence that has in fact occurred and is the news itself.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Emphasis framing (Vurgu çerçevelemesi)

`emphasis-framing` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

Outlets covering the same fact foreground different dimensions of it in their headlines.

**All of these must hold:**

1. Two or more outlets in the text report the same underlying fact.
2. Their headlines foreground different dimensions of that fact (cost versus benefit, who gains versus who loses, number versus person).

**Not this:** Different emphasis that follows from the outlets reporting different facts or different moments of a developing story.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Episodic framing (Olay odaklı çerçeveleme)

`episodic-framing` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

The text presents a recurring or structural issue through a single case or person, without the conditions that produce it.

**All of these must hold:**

1. The supplied context or the other outlets' coverage shows the issue is recurring or structural (earlier cases, figures, an institutional process).
2. The text presents one case or person as the whole story.
3. Neither headline nor lede refers to the wider pattern.

**Not this:** A first report of a breaking incident, where the wider pattern is not yet known or not yet relevant.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## False dilemma (Yanlış ikilem)

`false-dilemma` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

The text reduces an issue to two mutually exclusive options although the supplied material shows others.

**All of these must hold:**

1. The outlet's own wording presents exactly two options as the only ones (either/or, ya… ya…, tek yol).
2. The supplied text or context shows at least one further option that was proposed or available.

**Not this:** A genuinely binary matter, such as a yes/no vote or a court's guilty/not-guilty ruling.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Fear appeal (Korkuya başvurma)

`fear-appeal` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

The text uses threat or disaster language that the evidence or probability it reports does not match.

**All of these must hold:**

1. The outlet's own wording uses threat or disaster vocabulary (felaket, kâbus, alarm, dehşet, tehlike çanları).
2. The text or context gives the scale, probability or source of the risk.
3. The vocabulary is out of proportion to that scale, or no scale or source is given at all.

**Not this:** A warning attributed to a named authority with the scale of the risk stated, such as an official storm alert.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Labeling (Etiketleme)

`labeling` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

Outlets call the same actor by different epithets or titles, and the label carries a judgement of the actor's legitimacy.

**All of these must hold:**

1. Two or more outlets in the text refer to the same actor.
2. They use different epithets or titles for that actor in their own voice.
3. At least one label carries an evaluation beyond identification (for example 'iş insanı' against 'holding patronu', 'şüpheli' against 'terörist').

**Not this:** Different but equally neutral identifiers, such as a full name in one outlet and an official title in another.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Loaded language (Duygu yüklü dil)

`loaded-language` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

The outlet's own evaluative words present the event positively or negatively beyond what the reported facts state.

**All of these must hold:**

1. The outlet's own wording contains evaluative words (skandal, rezalet, müjde, tarihi, şok).
2. The same event is reported by another outlet in the text, or could be stated, without those words and with the same facts.

**Not this:** Reporting a negative or positive event in plain terms: a death, a defeat or a record is not loaded language by itself.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Metaphorical framing (Mecazi çerçeveleme)

`metaphorical-framing` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

The text describes the event through a metaphor that brings its own logic — war, disease, flood, game — and so shapes how the event is understood.

**All of these must hold:**

1. The outlet's own wording uses a metaphor for the event or the actors (savaş, kuşatma, tsunami, virüs, satranç).
2. The metaphor is not a fixed idiom: it assigns roles or implies a course of action (an enemy, a cure, a defence).

**Not this:** Dead metaphors and fixed idioms that no longer carry an image, such as 'faiz indirimi' or 'zam yağmuru' used as a set phrase in every outlet.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Attribution of responsibility framing (Sorumluluk atfı çerçevelemesi)

`responsibility-framing` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

The headline's grammar settles who is responsible before the process that would establish it is complete.

**All of these must hold:**

1. The text concerns an event whose responsibility is undetermined in the supplied material (an investigation, trial or inquiry is ongoing or not yet begun).
2. The outlet's own headline grammar assigns agency or blame to a named actor (active voice with the actor as subject, or 'X'in ihmali').
3. No attribution marker (iddia, öne sürüldü, savcılığa göre) accompanies the assignment.

**Not this:** Responsibility established by a final court ruling or an official finding that the text names.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Selective statistics (Seçici istatistik)

`selective-statistics` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

A media presentation pattern in which a rate or share is reported against a narrower denominator than the population the surrounding claim is about, while documented material shows a broader comparable population. The pattern concerns what the text presents; it says nothing about why, and a subgroup figure is not in itself an error.

**All of these must hold:**

1. The text presents a rate or share and its numerator and denominator can be identified from the text or context.
2. The supplied context documents a different comparison population and shows it is comparable to the one used.
3. The text's claim extends beyond the population its denominator covers; a claim explicitly confined to the subgroup does not qualify.

**Not this:** No comparison population is documented in the supplied material; the pattern cannot be assessed.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Spin (Genel eğilim)

`spin` · policy: `effect_context_only`

Several presentation choices in one text — selection, labels, loaded words, emphasis — point the same way and give the story an overall direction.

**All of these must hold:**

1. At least two distinct presentation choices are identifiable in the same outlet's headline and lede (for example an evaluative label and a selected figure).
2. They point in the same evaluative direction.
3. Another outlet in the text reports the same facts without that combination.

**Not this:** A single presentation choice; that belongs under its own concept, not here.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Strawman (Korkuluk safsatası)

`strawman` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

The text restates a position in a weaker or more extreme form than the one the supplied material shows was actually stated, and then criticises that version.

**All of these must hold:**

1. The supplied text or context contains the position as it was actually stated.
2. The outlet's own wording restates it in a more extreme or weaker form.
3. The criticism in the text is aimed at the restated version.

**Not this:** A fair paraphrase that shortens a statement without changing its scope.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Thematic framing (Bağlamsal çerçeveleme)

`thematic-framing` · policy: `effect_context_only`

The text places an event inside a trend, process or institutional context. This is not a flaw in itself; which context is chosen shapes the reading.

**All of these must hold:**

1. An identifiable event is the news.
2. The outlet's own headline or lede places it in a wider trend, series or institutional context (figures over time, earlier cases, a policy process).

**Not this:** Background added by an agency wire that every outlet in the text carries in identical words.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Word choice (Sözcük seçimi)

`word-choice` · policy: `media_pattern_review`

Outlets use words with different connotations for the same fact.

**All of these must hold:**

1. Two or more outlets in the text report the same fact.
2. They use different words for the same action or thing in their own voice (zam / fiyat güncellemesi, operasyon / baskın, göçmen / kaçak).
3. The words differ in connotation, not only in register or length.

**Not this:** Synonyms with no difference in connotation, or a technical term against its everyday equivalent.

Decision: ☐ approve ☐ edit ☐ drop

## Not measurable from headlines and ledes

These nine concepts need full articles, images or page layout and have no card: source selection, one-sided sourcing,
commission and omission of information, contextual omission, size allocation, story placement, picture selection,
picture explanation, event selection.
