# Declinism implementation note

This is an explicitly hypothetical, synthetic-only process model; it is not empirically validated and is not a language detector. The task supplies chronological 0–100 scores for at least two comparable domains, with at least two prior observations in state. For each domain, let `m` be the mean of prior observations, `p` their maximum, `x` the current score, and `s` the intervention strength. The modeled remembered past is `r = m + s(p - m)`, a peak-biased retrieval process rather than a generic penalty.

**Task and metric:** estimate the share of domains judged to be deteriorating at the current step. At strength zero, the explicit reference policy counts domains with `x < m`; the metric is that count divided by the number of domains. The model may add a domain only when `x >= m` but `p > x`, and only if at least two domains have this unsupported peak-versus-current contrast. This gate implements the supplied boundary: a supported decline in one measure is not generalized. At positive strength, the added judgment also requires `r > x`. The returned state retains the full chronological score history and per-step domain decisions.

The positive scenario has three domains whose current scores equal their prior means but fall below prior peaks, so at strength 0.8 all three are judged deteriorating (metric 1 versus reference 0). The boundary scenario has one supported declining health measure and two constant measures; its metric remains 1/3 at strength 0 and 0.8. Both scenarios use two current chronological contexts and carry state between them.

Assumptions and limitations: scores are comparable, positive-direction measures on a fixed scale; history is accurate; peak retrieval is a deliberately simple assumed process; and two domains is a toy operational threshold, not a population or clinical criterion. Legitimate peak-to-present declines, measurement noise, domain selection, and changing measurement scales are not resolved. Tests check software behavior only, not empirical validity.
