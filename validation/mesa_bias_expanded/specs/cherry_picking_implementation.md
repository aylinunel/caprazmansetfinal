# Cherry-picking mechanism implementation note

This is an explicitly hypothetical, structured-input process model; it is not empirically validated, a classifier, or a claim about prevalence or motive. It simulates one publisher choosing from supplied evidence records under a fixed `publication_slots` constraint. Each record must supply a unique ID, date label, direction (`supports` or `limits`), and relevance in `[0, 1]`.

**Process and metric.** The reference rule sorts records by supplied relevance (descending; ties by input order) and selects the first `publication_slots`. At strength `s`, if and only if both supporting and limiting records are available, supporting records receive ranking score `relevance × (1 + s)` and limiting records retain score `relevance`; the highest-scoring records fill the same slots (ties use raw relevance, then input order). The primary metric is the number of selected supporting records divided by slots, in `[0, 1]`. The state records the last selection, dates, support share, and omitted limiting IDs. `active` means that a positive strength changed the selected ID sequence from the strength-zero reference.

**Necessary condition and scenarios.** The modeled selective presentation requires competing evidence in both directions. The positive scenario has four records and two slots: at strength zero, relevance selects one limiting and one supporting item (support share `0.5`); at `0.8`, both supporting items rank first (share `1.0`). The boundary scenario removes all limiting evidence; strength cannot change the selection rule or primary metric, which remains `1.0` and matches the reference. At strength zero, the mechanism explicitly reproduces the relevance-only reference policy.

**Scope and limitations.** Dates and evidence directions are supplied facts; no natural-language or image analysis occurs. Relevance and the strength multiplier are illustrative assumptions, not fitted effect sizes. A constrained or selective presentation may be justified, and a higher presented support share does not establish falsehood, harm, or intent. The model does not assess source quality, representativeness, evidentiary weight, claim truth, or audience effects.
