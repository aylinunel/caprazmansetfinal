# Spin mechanism implementation note

This is an explicitly hypothetical `media_practice` process model, not an empirical claim, classifier, or detector. The task is to calculate a story's presented tone from supplied structured evidence: each evidence item has a direction in `[-1, 1]` and a positive weight. The primary metric is the dimensionless, evidence-weighted tone index in `[-1, 1]`.

The reference policy is the weighted factual mean, `sum(weight × direction) / sum(weight)`. Each explicitly supplied presentation form names a distinct form kind, focuses an evidence item, and supplies emphasis in `[0, 1]`. A convergent framing condition exists only when at least two distinct forms focus on evidence in the same nonzero direction, and that direction has a strict plurality among form choices. At strength `s`, each focused fact aligned with that direction receives weight `weight × (1 + s × sum(aligned form emphasis))`; other fact weights are unchanged. The reported metric is the weighted mean after those adjustments. `active` is true only when this changes the reference metric.

The positive scenario uses two forms (headline and ordering) to emphasize positive-direction evidence, shifting tone upward. The boundary scenario keeps the same evidence but has only one form, removing the necessary cross-form convergence; its metric equals the reference at strength 0. At strength 0 the rule always reproduces the weighted-mean reference.

Assumptions and limitations: supplied evidence directions and weights and authored form choices are synthetic, not inferred from natural-language content. Distinct form kinds stand in for independently chosen presentation forms; linear salience increments and the two-form threshold are illustrative user-settable assumptions, not measured coefficients. The model captures only this narrow convergence mechanism, does not infer motive, deception, or reader response, and cannot distinguish legitimate editorial emphasis from misleading emphasis. No human validation or detection accuracy is claimed.
