# The Sunk Cost Fallacy — illustrative implementation

This module represents one explicit continue-or-exit choice. Inputs provide past expenditure, the amount still recoverable, and comparable actual future utilities for continuing and exiting. It computes `sunk = past_expenditure - recoverable_amount`; the reference policy selects the option with the greater future utility and exits on a tie. At strength `s`, the modeled continuation score is `future_value_continue + s * sunk`, while the exit score remains `future_value_exit`. The selected option's **actual future utility** is the primary metric, in the same utility units as the two input values. State carries chronological choices and their actual utilities; no extra simulation steps are invented.

Necessary conditions are explicit numeric evidence of positive unrecoverable expenditure and a decision in which that amount can influence continuation. Recoverable value is not sunk. Any future contractual payments or consequences belong in the appropriate supplied future-value estimate, not in past expenditure. Missing or inconsistent evidence and invalid ranges raise `ValueError`.

The positive scenario has 5 units of entirely unrecoverable past expenditure, future continuation utility -2, and exit utility 0: at strength .8, the continuation score becomes 2. The reference exits with metric 0; the mechanism continues with metric -2. The boundary scenario keeps past expenditure at 5 but makes all 5 recoverable, so the mechanism adds zero and matches the reference metric.

All coefficients and values are illustrative user-set assumptions, not effect sizes. This is a synthetic process model only: it is not empirically validated, fitted, a classifier, or evidence about prevalence or actual decision makers. It assumes comparable future-value inputs and does not infer hidden motives or recoverability from narrative text; real decisions may include uncertainty, learning, option value, non-monetary consequences, or rational reasons correlated with prior spending.
