# Episodic Framing — illustrative implementation

This plugin models an authored coverage choice, not a reader's belief or an NLP classifier. For a supplied context budget `B`, event relevance `E`, and structural-context relevance `S`, its explicit strength-zero reference policy assigns structural context `B·S/(S+E)` units. When (and only when) the supplied facts indicate both a single-event focus and evidence that structural causes produce the issue, the illustrative mechanism increases the event's allocation weight to `E·(1+strength)`. The reported metric is then `B·S/(S+E·(1+strength))`. If either necessary condition is absent, event weight stays `E`, so the primary metric equals the reference.

The task is the division of a finite authored context budget; metric units are illustrative structural-context units, not words, factuality, quality, or harm. Inputs explicitly supply relevance weights and a causal-evidence flag; the model infers neither from language. The positive scenario supplies both necessary conditions and should reduce structural allocation at strength `.8`. The boundary scenario removes causal-structure evidence and must match the reference. The reference policy is a simple proportional allocation, not a claim of perfect editorial judgment.

All coefficients are illustrative assumptions, not published effect sizes. This model is explicitly hypothetical and not empirically validated. It cannot determine whether a real story is misleading: event-focused coverage may be appropriate, and structural context may appear elsewhere. It excludes audience effects, competing story constraints, and all concepts other than this narrow event-versus-structure allocation.
