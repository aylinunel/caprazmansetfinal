# Bikeshedding mechanism implementation

This illustrative process allocates a fixed discussion budget over agenda issues with supplied consequence (`c`) and ease-of-discussion (`e`) ratings. The strength-zero reference policy distributes attention proportionally to consequence: `a_i = B c_i / Σc`. At positive strength `s`, and only when the agenda contains a pair with `c_low < c_high` and `e_low > e_high`, the issue weights become `w_i = c_i exp(s e_i (1-c_i))` and attention is `a_i = B w_i / Σw`. The implementation stores per-issue attention and cumulative state without altering caller-owned inputs.

The primary metric is `Σ(a_i c_i) / B`, an attention-weighted mean consequence rating on the supplied 0–1 scale. A lower value indicates that more discussion budget went to lower-consequence agenda items; it is not a belief score or a judgment of the discussion's overall quality.

The necessary condition is an explicitly rated inverse ease/consequence pair: an easier, lower-consequence issue competing with a harder, higher-consequence issue. If this relationship is absent, the mechanism uses the reference allocation exactly, including the boundary case where a seemingly small issue has higher ease but is rated consequential (no inverse pair). Missing, non-finite, or out-of-range task evidence raises `ValueError` rather than yielding a zero effect.

The positive scenario contrasts an easy minor formatting question with a difficult safety review; the boundary scenario has equal ease ratings and therefore no qualifying pair. Consequence and ease ratings are inputs, never inferred from labels or prose. Formula, coefficients, ratings, and activation condition are explicit illustrative assumptions, not published effect sizes. This model is synthetic and explicitly not empirically validated; it is not an NLP detector, diagnosis, population claim, or claim about discussion motives. Actual small details may warrant substantial time when their consequences are high, and real deliberation may depend on omitted constraints, dependencies, expertise, or coordination costs.
