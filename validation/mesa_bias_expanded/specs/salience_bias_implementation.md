# Salience bias implementation note

This synthetic, unvalidated process model allocates a supplied finite attention budget across structured evidence items. For item *i*, its weight is `relevance_i + strength × max(relevance) × max(0, prominence_i − mean(prominence))`; attention shares normalize the weights, then multiply by the budget. The primary metric is relevance-weighted attention quality, `sum(relevance_i × allocated_attention_i) / attention_budget`, in mean supplied relevance units (0–1) per attention unit. The complete per-round allocation and metric are retained in state.

At strength zero, the explicit reference distributes attention in proportion to supplied relevance. Salience requires differing prominence: an item's positive excess over package-mean prominence adds attraction. The positive scenario has a quiet, highly relevant item (0.9) and a conspicuous, weakly relevant item (0.1), so strength 0.8 diverts attention and lowers the metric. The boundary scenario gives both items prominence 0.7; with no relative prominence excess, strength 0.8 exactly recovers the reference primary metric. This models prominence, not broader attentional bias arising from internal concerns.

Relevance and prominence are explicit authored inputs, not inferred from source text or images; attention is continuous and fully allocated. Coefficients are illustrative assumptions, not measured effect sizes. The mechanism is not an empirical account, classifier, diagnosis, or fairness judgment. Conspicuous evidence can be useful, and actual attention may reflect context, motivation, cognitive limits, and other benign reasons not represented here. No human validation or detection accuracy is claimed.
